Here is my straightforward take. The location is the biggest selling point. If you attend UMD, being right across from campus is undeniably convenient. Unfortunately, most of the positives stop there.
The elevator situation is a daily obstacle course. Out of the three in the lobby, only one or two bother to function, and the rightmost one appears to have retired permanently. Living on the 16th floor means budgeting extra time just to avoid being late.
Maintenance requests feel like a game of repetition: submit the same report two or three times before anyone takes action, and even then, the fixes are partial at best. There is a peeling yellow patch in our shower that has been completely ignored.
Leasing information was inconsistent from the start. We were first told that 2×2 units were available and that we could likely have a 4×2 to ourselves due to low occupancy. When we emailed to confirm, we were instead told everything was almost full and roommates were guaranteed. Even the cost of insurance was misrepresented. The only thing the tour guide accurately stated was the base rent.
Noise insulation might as well not exist. From scooters riding across the bridge to people chatting on the sidewalk, everything is clearly audible on the 16th floor. On top of that, dust accumulates at a supernatural pace, likely due to poorly maintained filters. The constant smell of weed in the hallway does not help the “fresh air” situation.
Missing packages and food deliveries are apparently your problem alone, even with cameras everywhere. If you pick up parcels immediately after the delivery notification, you still get told to return later.
In summary: great location, but nearly every essential living factor falls short. If this is premium student housing, the bar needs more than a lift… it needs an elevator that actually works.

The entire building lost internet for a full day, leaving everyone unable to work or study, which is completely unacceptable given the monthly rent of $1,200 to $1,600 or more. The units feel cramped, and the pricing makes the shared bathroom situation even harder to justify.
While some people say this is still “better than the Varsity,” that feels like winning the world’s saddest competition. The overall apartment situation in College Park seems downright discouraging. If possible, consider living farther out and driving in, or even attending a different school nearby if you want housing that matches the price you pay.
College housing should not feel like paying premium prices to live in a shoebox with unstable basics.
